Privacy Policy

RenTab — Rename Tab. Last updated: 8 November 2026.

RenTab lets you give any browser tab a title of your own. This page explains exactly what the extension stores, what it sends, and what it never touches.

Your titles

  • The titles you type and the addresses they belong to are stored in your own browser, using Chrome's extension storage.
  • If you turn on Chrome Sync in Settings, Chrome carries them between your own signed-in devices through your Google account. They travel inside Chrome's own sync, not through any server of ours.
  • We operate no server that receives your titles or the addresses of the pages you visit. There is nowhere for us to read them, even if we wanted to.
  • Export writes this data to a file you choose. Import reads one back. Nothing leaves your computer in either direction.

Pages you visit

To replace a tab's title, the extension needs to run a short script on the page that is open. That is what the "Read and change all your data on the websites you visit" permission is for. The script does one thing: it sets the page title. It does not read page content, form fields or anything you type, and it sends nothing anywhere.

Anonymous usage statistics

To see which features are actually used, the extension sends anonymous events to PostHog, a product analytics service. An event is the name of an action and a few coarse details.

What is sent:

  • the action: for example title_saved, color_set, export, history_entry_opened;
  • the interface language, the extension version, whether Chrome Sync is on, and whether the title could be applied to the tab;
  • a size bucket such as 6-20 for how many titles you have saved — never the exact number;
  • a random identifier stored on this device, so that repeat use counts as one person. It is not linked to your name, your Google account or any other service;
  • a coarse location that PostHog estimates from the IP address of the request (see below).

What is never sent:

  • the address of any page, or any part of it;
  • the text of any title you write;
  • anything typed into the search box;
  • the contents of an export.

PostHog receives your IP address as part of the request, as any web server does. It is used to estimate a coarse location, so that usage can be compared between countries. We do not send your location, and the extension itself never reads it.

There is no autocapture, no session recording, no surveys, no feature flags, and no code is loaded from PostHog's servers — Chrome's extension rules forbid remote code and nothing here needs it.

Switching it off: Settings → "Anonymous usage stats". When you turn it off, nothing further is sent and the random identifier is deleted from your device.

Tab colour

Colouring a tab is done by putting that tab in a tab group of its own and colouring the group, which is the only way Chrome allows. That needs the tabGroups permission, which is optional: it is requested the first time you pick a colour, and never on install. If a tab is already part of a group you made yourself, the extension leaves it alone rather than recolour your other tabs.

What we do not do

  • We do not sell or share personal data with anyone.
  • There is no advertising and no cross-site tracking. The extension does not follow you between websites and does not build a profile of your browsing.
  • There is no account and no sign-in.
  • The statistics above are used only to decide what to improve.

Permissions

  • storage — keeps your titles and settings in the browser.
  • scripting and access to websites — sets the title on the open page.
  • contextMenus — adds "Set title" to the extension's right-click menu.
  • tabGroups (optional) — colours a tab, asked for only if you use colours.
  • Allow access to file URLs — not requested by the extension. Chrome keeps local files behind a switch you turn on yourself, on the extension's details page, if you want to rename file:// pages.

Children

RenTab is not directed at children and collects nothing that could identify anyone.
